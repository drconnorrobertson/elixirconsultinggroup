The Hidden Value in Boring Businesses: Where Smart Acquirers Look

March 19, 2026 By Connor Robertson

I'm going to tell you something that might seem counterintuitive: the best acquisitions I've ever seen weren't glamorous. They were boring. They were the kind of businesses that don't make headlines, that don't have venture capital backing, that don't have a sexy story to tell at a cocktail party. They were waste management companies, commercial cleaning services, local HVAC contractors, equipment rental firms, and niche manufacturing operations.

While everyone else in the acquisition space was chasing technology startups and trending SaaS companies, the smartest acquirers I know were busy building wealth through boring businesses. And they were getting rich doing it.

Why Boring is Actually Beautiful

Let me explain why boring businesses are where the real money is. First, boring means predictable. A waste management company doesn't surprise you. It has recurring revenue. Its customers don't wake up one day and decide they no longer need waste removal. Boring businesses have products and services that are essential, not optional.

I worked with an acquirer who bought a small commercial cleaning company for $800,000. On the surface, it wasn't impressive. It had 20 employees, serviced about 40 commercial clients, and made around $180,000 a year in profit. Not life-changing numbers. But the acquirer saw something nobody else did: this business had 15-year contracts with major office buildings. The customers weren't going anywhere. The revenue was absolutely predictable.

Six years later, that same acquirer had acquired five similar cleaning companies and rolled them into a regional operation. He sold the entire portfolio for over $12 million. He turned boring into a fortune.

The Psychology That Works Against You

Here's the problem: most acquirers are psychologically wired to avoid boring businesses. They want to find the next big thing. They want to own something they can brag about. They want an acquisition story that excites their peers. Nobody gets excited at a networking event about acquiring a dumpster rental company. But you know what? The person who acquired five dumpster rental companies just became very wealthy.

This psychological bias is incredibly valuable if you're willing to think differently. While everyone else is chasing growth stories, you can be acquiring stable, profitable, boring businesses at reasonable valuations. The competition is low, the valuations are fair, and the predictability is high.

Boring businesses also have less financial engineering. They're not using complicated cap tables or venture debt. They don't have multiple classes of stock. The finances are usually straightforward, which means your due diligence is simpler and your understanding of what you're buying is clearer.

The Boring Business Advantage

There are several specific advantages to acquiring boring businesses that most acquirers overlook:

Owner Motivation. Most boring business owners aren't trying to build the next unicorn. They're trying to exit their business because they're tired, or they want to retire, or they need capital for something else. This often means they're willing to be reasonable about valuations and terms. They don't have unrealistic expectations. They understand their business's limitations. This makes negotiation easier and often results in better deal terms.

Customer Stickiness. Because boring businesses provide essential services, customer churn is typically low. A new HVAC contractor company isn't going to steal all your customers overnight just because they're cheaper. People don't switch vendors for essential services easily. This means your customer relationships are sticky, and your revenue is predictable.

Low Technology Risk. Many boring businesses aren't disrupted by technology because the core service is inherently local or manual. A plumbing company can't be disrupted by an app. A janitorial service needs people. This means you're not competing with some venture-funded competitor who might out-innovate you. Your business will be relevant in five years the same way it's relevant today.

Acquisition and Consolidation Opportunities. The boring business space is incredibly fragmented. Most markets have dozens or hundreds of small competitors. This creates huge consolidation opportunities. You can acquire multiple small competitors, run them under one umbrella, benefit from economies of scale, and create a much more valuable business. Many of the wealthiest entrepreneurs I know built their fortunes through this exact strategy.

How to Identify Boring Business Opportunities

If you're going to pursue boring businesses, you need to know what to look for. Here are the characteristics of a good boring business acquisition target:

Recurring Revenue. The best boring businesses have customers who pay regularly. Monthly subscriptions, long-term contracts, annual maintenance agreements. This eliminates the feast-and-famine cycle that kills many businesses.

Low Customer Concentration. If the business has one customer that represents 50% of revenue, that's a risk. You want to see revenue distributed across many customers so that losing one doesn't crater your business.

Skilled Labor or Expertise. The best boring businesses have some barrier to entry. Maybe they require specialized skills. Maybe they require licensing or certifications. Maybe they require relationships that take years to build. This gives the business protection from competition.

Reasonable Debt Load. A boring business shouldn't be leveraged to the hilt. You want to see a business with clean financials and a reasonable amount of debt. This gives you flexibility to add more debt for acquisitions and consolidation.

Owner Willingness to Stay. The best boring business acquisitions include a period where the original owner stays on. They know the customers. They know the operations. Having them involved during transition de-risks the acquisition significantly.

The Real Wealth Builder

Here's what I've learned from working with successful acquirers: wealth isn't built by finding the next big thing. It's built by systematically acquiring boring, profitable businesses and consolidating them into larger, more valuable operations. It's not as exciting as the venture capital narrative, but it's far more reliable.

If you're looking to build real wealth through acquisitions, I'd encourage you to spend less time looking at trendy SaaS companies and more time looking at the boring businesses operating in your backyard. They might not make for great stories, but they make for excellent returns.

For a deeper dive into this strategy, check out my book Creative Acquisitions, which explores unconventional acquisition approaches and how to find value where others don't see it. The same principles that work for boring businesses apply to many other overlooked opportunities in the acquisition space.

The path to wealth through acquisitions isn't always glamorous. Sometimes it's just work, discipline, and the willingness to do what everyone else thinks is boring. And that's exactly why it works.
